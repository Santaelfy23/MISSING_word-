How to tackle missing letters
  1. Read the whole sentence first, and the one before. The meaning usually gives the word away.
  2. In the exam you only type the next two letters. If they’re right, the word fills itself in. If nothing happens, those two letters are wrong: think of another word that fits and try again.
  3. Skip and come back. You don’t have to go in order. Do the ones you know first, then return to the hard ones.
  4. Check the ending. “was” or “were” often needs -ed or -ing. “Three” or “a few” needs a plural -s. A word saying how often ends -ly.
  5. Get the first two letters exactly right. Watch tricky starts like ph- (pharaoh), wr- (wrench), kn-, rh- (rhythm) and double letters (succeeded).
  6. Easy ones first, never leave a gap empty. Every gap is worth one mark. A guess can score; a blank can’t.
  7. Newspaper articles use formal reporting words: residents, announced, according to, spokesperson, confirmed. Expect them.
  8. Reread with your word in. If it sounds wrong, it probably is.
